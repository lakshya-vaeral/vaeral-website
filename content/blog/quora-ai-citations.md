**Quora is the sixth most-cited domain in Google AI Overviews**, with a 4.7% share of citations across more than three million US queries in [Ahrefs' September 2026 analysis](https://ahrefs.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai-overviews/). YouTube leads at 22.9%.

That is interesting on its own. It becomes useful because of a second finding: AI engines increasingly cite pages that are not ranking well.

## Why does being cited no longer require ranking?

In 2025, being cited in an AI Overview mostly meant you already ranked. By 2026 that had stopped being true: [Ahrefs' March 2026 study of 863,000 search results pages and four million AI Overview URLs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10) found only 37.1% of cited URLs also ranked in the organic top ten for the same query, down from 76% in its previous analysis.

The practical reading is that the AI answer and the ten blue links are no longer the same contest. A page can be pulled into an answer without winning the ranking, and a page can win the ranking and never be pulled in.

## What makes a Quora answer easy to cite?

Quora content is already the shape a retrieval system is looking for.

- The question is the heading, written the way a person actually asks it.
- The answer starts immediately underneath, with no preamble.
- The author is named, with a stated background.
- Competing answers sit on the same page, which reads as corroboration rather than a single claim.

A service page has none of this. It is written to sell, it opens with positioning, and it has one voice. For a question like "which agency handles negative Quora answers", a page selling the service is a worse structural match than a page answering the question.

This is the same principle behind [Answer Engine Optimisation](/blog/what-is-aeo): the format of the content decides whether it can be extracted, separately from whether it deserves to be.

## Where this actually helps

Two cases, and they are narrower than the opportunity usually gets described as.

**A question already exists and already gets traffic.** Answering it well is a genuine route into AI answers for a term your own site is nowhere near. The question was going to be cited by someone.

**A question about your brand has one bad answer on it.** A single unanswered complaint can become the cited source for your company name. Adding a better answer does not delete it, but it changes what the page as a whole says. This is the same mechanic as [handling a negative Reddit thread](/blog/remove-reddit-post-google), on a platform that gets cited more often.

What it does not do is build an asset. A citation on someone else's platform is a borrowed position.

## What we will not do

Quora's own rule is that [a user should disclose any relevant affiliations explicitly in an answer or in the credential that appears with the answer](https://help.quora.com/hc/en-us/articles/360055133711-What-is-Quora-s-policy-on-disclosing-affiliations-in-answers). Writing about your own company without saying so breaks it.

So: no undisclosed answers, no sockpuppet accounts, no upvote rings. Not as a moral position, but because the citation is the point and a removed answer cites nothing. An account caught doing this loses every answer attached to it, including the ones that were working.

## The honest summary

Quora is cited often, and citation no longer depends on your own rankings. That makes it a real surface and a cheap one to test.

It is also a surface you do not own, measured in a channel with no reliable attribution. Treat it as one route into AI answers alongside your own pages, not as a substitute for them.
