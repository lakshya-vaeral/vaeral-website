sectionOneHeading: "What this service is"
sectionOne: |
  LinkedIn personal branding is the work of making a founder or executive legible and credible to the people who evaluate their company — buyers, investors, journalists, and prospective hires.

  For most B2B companies the founder's profile is checked more often than the company page. It is where a prospect goes after a first call, where a journalist goes before quoting you, and where a candidate goes before accepting an offer. It is also, increasingly, a source that answer engines draw on when asked who runs a company or who the notable people in an industry are.

  This service covers positioning, the profile itself, and a posting practice the founder can actually sustain.

sectionTwoHeading: "How it works"
sectionTwo: |
  ### 1. Establish the position

  What is this person credibly the person to talk about? The answer has to be narrow enough to be memorable and true enough to survive questioning. Founders who post about everything are read as posting about nothing.

  ### 2. Rebuild the profile

  Headline, about section, featured work and experience, written for the two audiences that matter: a human deciding whether to take you seriously in the eight seconds they spend, and LinkedIn's search, which is how people find you for a topic rather than by name.

  ### 3. Set a cadence that survives contact with a real schedule

  Most founder LinkedIn efforts fail at week six, because the plan assumed time nobody has. We set a frequency the founder can actually hold — often weekly rather than daily — and build a repeatable process for producing it.

  ### 4. Develop the voice, from the founder's actual thinking

  We work from interviews, recorded calls, internal notes and things the founder already says in meetings. The output has to sound like them, because their team, customers and investors all know what they sound like.

  ### 5. Engage, not just publish

  Substantive comments on other people's posts frequently reach further than original posts, and they build the relationships that make later posts travel. This is the part most people skip.

sectionThreeHeading: "Who it's for"
sectionThree: |
  **This is a fit if** you are a founder or executive in a category where trust drives the deal — B2B software, professional services, healthcare, finance — and you have genuine expertise you have simply never made visible.

  **This is not a fit if** you want a ghostwriter to invent opinions you do not hold. That approach breaks the first time someone asks you about a post in a meeting. It is also not a fit if you cannot commit any time at all: the work requires the founder's actual thinking as raw material, even when the drafting is done for them. Perhaps thirty minutes a week is the realistic floor.

  We are also a poor fit for engagement-farming tactics — the manufactured-vulnerability posts and comment pods that inflate numbers while quietly costing credibility with the specific audience you need.

  ### Related work

  Founder visibility often produces the independent coverage that [Wikipedia notability](/services/wikipedia-page-creation) requires. It also pairs with [AI search visibility](/services/ai-search-visibility), since a well-documented founder makes a company easier for answer engines to resolve.
