sectionOneHeading: "What download and signup growth is"
sectionOne: |
  Download and signup growth is the work of closing the gap between the people who already consider you and the people who actually complete — installing the app, or finishing the signup.

  It is not paid acquisition. There is no media buying here, no cost-per-install target and no budget being spent to put your listing in front of more people. The premise is the opposite: most brands have more consideration than they convert, and the cheapest growth available is fixing the reasons that people who found you decided against you.

  The scope is the store listing and the handful of lines a visitor genuinely reads, the rating that gates the decision before the copy is even considered, the searches people run in the days before installing, and the same problem on the web, where the completion is a signup rather than an install.
sectionTwoHeading: "How it works"
sectionTwo: |
  ### 1. Establish where the drop happens

  Impressions, store page views, installs and first-week retention are four different problems with four different fixes. Treating them as one number is the reason most listing work produces nothing: a brand rewrites its screenshots when the actual loss was happening a step earlier, or a step later.

  ### 2. Fix what the listing says and shows

  Title, subtitle, description and screenshots, judged by what a visitor reads in the seconds before deciding — which is far less than what was written. Most listings describe the product to someone who already understands the category, and lose everyone who does not.

  ### 3. Address the rating, because it is the gate

  No amount of listing copy out-argues a rating a visitor does not trust. Where the rating is the constraint, that is the work, and it belongs to review and response management rather than to anything on the store page.

  ### 4. Own the searches that happen before the store

  People check before they install. "Is it safe", "is it legit", "review", "alternative" — asked on search engines, in communities, and increasingly of answer engines that summarise all three. What those searches return is part of the install decision even though none of it happens on your listing.

  ### 5. Measure installs against retention, not on their own

  Installs that uninstall within the week cost you store standing and tell you the promise and the product disagree. A rise in installs alongside a fall in retention is not growth, and reporting the first without the second is how growth work gets to look successful while getting more expensive.
sectionThreeHeading: "Who it's for"
sectionThree: |
  **This is a fit if** you have a product with real usage and a listing or funnel that undersells it, a rating that is visibly suppressing installs, or you are entering a category where buyers check communities and AI answers before they ever open the store.

  **This is not a fit if** you want install and signup numbers rather than customers. Incentivised installs and install exchanges breach the Play Store and App Store developer policies, churn within days, damage store standing through the retention signals they produce, and put the listing itself at risk of removal. Nobody can guarantee an install count, and anyone quoting one is describing paid media with a different label on it.

  It is also the wrong service for an app that loses people inside the first session. Growth work amplifies whatever the product currently does, including the leak, so the honest sequence is onboarding first. And it will not manufacture a difference that is not there: if the category is crowded and the product is genuinely undifferentiated, a listing cannot invent a reason to choose it.
